# When the Evening Comes



The gate did not creak today.

It opened smoothly,

like it didn’t notice

everything I was carrying.



The corridor didn’t echo,

but my footsteps knew

they’d never sound the same again.



The air is still, too quiet for a place

which formerly knew

how my laughter once landed.



A few books filled,

some creased old notes

with jagged edges torn from careless fingers—

none of that seems like enough.



I tarried a bit longer on the benches under the tree

saw dust suspended in the sun.

Nobody said my name.

No one had to.



Evenings will come—

light falling soft on the road by the river,

And all will be the same.

Except—

the sky may feel heavier,

and one seat might stay untouched.

They won’t know.

They shouldn’t.

But perhaps the wind will pause,

just for a second,

like it forgot

what it was supposed to do.
