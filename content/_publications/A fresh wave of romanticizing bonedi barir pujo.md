A fresh wave of romanticizing bonedi barir pujo seems to be resurfacing. The aesthetics are undeniably captivating, but the history behind many of these households is often left out of the conversation.



I'm not trying to make a class-war argument. My point is simply that many of these bonedi baris emerged from social structures that were deeply hierarchical. In numerous cases, Durga Puja was an exclusive affair rather than a sarbojonin one, with access and participation shaped by caste, class, and status. The idea of everyone celebrating together came much later through the rise of community pujas, or in some cases freedom struggles. 



There's a certain irony in seeing these spaces today become backdrops for aesthetic reels, inter-caste couples, and crowds from every walk of life. If some of the ancestors who once guarded these rituals as symbols of privilege could witness it now, they might be astonished to find that the very courtyards they sought to keep exclusive have become spaces where social boundaries blur.



History has a way of reclaiming places in ways their builders never imagined.
